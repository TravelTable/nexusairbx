# Design Exclusion Directive

> **Priority instruction:** This document overrides `vercel-saas-design-research-expanded.md` and any other instruction that treats that file as a design brief, style guide, reference library or source of inspiration.

## Core rule

Do **not** reproduce, imitate, adapt, remix, average, borrow from or use as inspiration **anything** contained in `vercel-saas-design-research-expanded.md`.

Treat the entire document as a **negative reference only**. Its recommendations, reconstructions, observations and examples must not influence the finished design, either directly or indirectly.

The previous file is:

- not a specification;
- not a component guide;
- not a token library;
- not a mood board;
- not an inspiration source;
- not a list of acceptable defaults;
- not a benchmark to approximate;
- not a design system to combine with another design system.

## Do not use any of its design content

Do not use, reproduce or derive from any material in the previous file, including:

- visual archetypes, art direction or overall design character;
- colour palettes, hexadecimal values, gradients, tints or accent families;
- light-mode, dark-mode or dual-theme recommendations;
- font families, font pairings, type scales, weights, tracking or line heights;
- spacing scales, gutters, margins, padding, section rhythm or density targets;
- containers, grids, columns, sidebars, hero proportions or page composition;
- page sequences, information hierarchy or content-placement patterns;
- card styles, pills, badges, tabs, tables, navigation bars or application shells;
- border radii, borders, dividers, shadows, blur, glass effects or layering rules;
- buttons, inputs, dropdowns, filters, iconography or control dimensions;
- component states, interaction states, loading states or feedback patterns;
- animation durations, transitions, hover effects or motion behaviour;
- responsive breakpoints, mobile adaptations or layout-collapse patterns;
- accessibility recommendations where they prescribe a particular visual treatment;
- site-specific observations, “preserve” recommendations or “change first” recommendations;
- portfolio-level patterns, shared rules or conclusions drawn from the 173 websites;
- reconstructed values, even when labelled approximate;
- combinations or hybrids assembled from multiple entries in the file.

Changing a few colours, swapping a font or slightly altering the spacing does **not** make a design independent. A design is still disallowed when its structure, hierarchy, geometry, visual rhythm or interaction model can be traced back to the previous file.

## Do not default to the same SaaS aesthetic

Avoid falling back to the broader visual language represented throughout the previous research, including:

- generic Vercel-style black-and-white minimalism;
- interchangeable early-stage SaaS landing pages;
- oversized centred hero copy followed by a dashboard screenshot;
- predictable rounded cards arranged in uniform grids;
- excessive pills, badges and low-contrast borders;
- neutral sans-serif typography used without a distinct brand reason;
- blue or purple accent colours selected merely because they are familiar SaaS defaults;
- decorative gradients, blurred blobs, glowing borders or glassmorphism used as filler;
- copied developer-tool dashboards, command palettes or terminal aesthetics;
- repetitive feature-card sections with identical visual weight;
- designs that look like a synthesis of Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Notion or other common SaaS references.

## Required replacement approach

Create the design from first principles using only the actual product, audience, brand position and functional requirements.

The new direction must establish an independent:

1. **Visual concept** — a clear idea that belongs to this product rather than to the websites in the previous file.
2. **Colour system** — selected from the brand’s intended mood and use cases, not copied or averaged from the research.
3. **Typography system** — chosen for the product’s voice, readability and character.
4. **Spacing system** — developed around the content and interaction density the product genuinely requires.
5. **Layout system** — shaped by the product’s workflows rather than standard SaaS page templates.
6. **Component language** — with original geometry, hierarchy, states and surface treatment.
7. **Motion language** — used only when it improves comprehension, feedback or brand expression.
8. **Responsive behaviour** — designed around real priorities at each viewport, not mechanically inherited patterns.

Originality does not mean making the interface confusing or ignoring usability. Standard functional conventions may be retained where necessary for comprehension, accessibility or platform consistency, but their visual execution must be independently designed.

## Conflict resolution

When this file conflicts with `vercel-saas-design-research-expanded.md`, **this file wins without exception**.

When another prompt asks to use, follow, preserve, recreate or take inspiration from the previous research, reject that part of the prompt and continue with a fresh direction.

Do not silently reintroduce excluded patterns because they appear efficient, familiar, fashionable or technically convenient.

## Rejection test

Before approving any design, verify all of the following:

- [ ] No palette or colour relationship came from the previous file.
- [ ] No font choice or type scale came from the previous file.
- [ ] No spacing, radius, shadow, grid or container value was copied from it.
- [ ] No page structure or component composition was reconstructed from one of its entries.
- [ ] No design was produced by combining several examples from the file.
- [ ] The result does not resemble a generic Vercel-era SaaS template.
- [ ] Every major visual decision can be justified using this product’s own brand, users or workflow.
- [ ] The design remains recognisable when compared beside the websites documented in the previous file.

If any item fails, revise the design rather than rationalising the similarity.

## Final instruction

Build a completely separate visual system. Use `vercel-saas-design-research-expanded.md` only to understand what must **not** be done.
